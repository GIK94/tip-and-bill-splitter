● Here are the calculation rules I'd propose for both split methods. Once you
  confirm them (and send the user flow and tech stack), I'll turn everything
  into the requirements doc.

  Shared rules (both methods)

  - All money math uses whole cents, never floating-point dollars. This rules
    out errors like 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004.
  - Bill amount (B): required. Must be more than $0.00 and at most $99,999.99,
    with at most 2 decimal places. It already includes tax.
  - Tip (T):
    - Percentage (presets 0 / 10 / 15 / 18 / 20, or a custom %): T = B × % /
      100, rounded half-up to the cent. Custom % must be between 0 and 100, with
      up to 2 decimal places.
    - Fixed amount: T is entered directly in dollars, at least $0.00, up to 2
      decimal places.
  - Grand total: G = B + T.
  - Each person's result shows three numbers: bill share, tip share and total.
    The bill shares add up to exactly B, the tip shares to exactly T, and the
    totals to exactly G.
  - Fair cent distribution (largest-remainder method):
    a. Work out each person's exact share.
    b. Round every share down to the cent.
    c. Hand out the cents left over, one at a time, to the people whose
       rounded-off fractions were largest. If two people tie, the person listed
       first gets the cent.

    The result: nobody is ever more than 1¢ away from their exact share, and the
    same inputs always give the same output.
  - How the three numbers are built: I split the total G with the method above,
    then split the tip T the same way, and set bill share = total share − tip
    share. This makes all three columns add up exactly at once.

  Method 1: Even split

  - Inputs: B, the tip, and the number of people N (a whole number from 1
    to 50).
  - Calculation: G is shared equally among the N people. Each person gets G ÷ N,
    rounded down to the cent. The leftover cents go +1¢ each to the first
    people in the list.

  Example: $100.00 bill, 18% tip, 3 people
  - Tip = $18.00 and total = $118.00
  - Totals: $39.34, $39.33, $39.33, which add up to $118.00 ✓
  - Tip shares: $6.00 each. Bill shares: $33.34, $33.33, $33.33.

  Edge cases
  - N = 1: that person pays everything.
  - 0% tip: only the bill is split.
  - A fixed tip larger than the bill is allowed, but the app shows a warning.
  - If the total is fewer cents than there are people (e.g. $0.02 split 3 ways),
    some people pay $0.00. This is mathematically correct, and B > 0 makes it
    rare.

  Method 2: Itemised split

  - Inputs:
    - B and the tip, as above
    - a list of people (names; default "Person 1", "Person 2", …)
    - a list of items, each with a price and one or more people assigned
  - Shared items: the item's price is divided equally among the people assigned
    to it.
  - Weights: each person's weight is the sum of their item portions. W = the sum
    of all item prices.
  - Proportional allocation: person i pays G × (weight_i / W) in total, and T ×
    (weight_i / W) in tip. Both are then distributed fairly to the cent.
  - Bill amount vs. items: B is the source of truth. The difference B − W (tax,
    service charge, etc.) is spread proportionally, as you specified, and the
    app shows it as "Tax & fees: $X."

  Example: B = $54.00, 20% tip
  - Items: Pasta $20 (Alice), Steak $25 (Bob), Fries $6 shared by Alice, Bob and
    Cara. W = $51.00.
  - Weights: Alice $22, Bob $27, Cara $2.
  - Tip = $10.80 and G = $64.80.
  - Totals: Alice $27.95, Bob $34.31, Cara $2.54, which add up to $64.80 ✓.
    Bob's exact share had the largest fraction, so he gets the leftover cent.
  - Tip shares: $4.66, $5.72, $0.42, which add up to $10.80 ✓
  - Bill shares: $23.29, $28.59, $2.12, which add up to $54.00 ✓
